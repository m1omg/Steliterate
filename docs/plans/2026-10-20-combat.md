# Combat: battles fought out, tactics, and neighbours who fight back (20 Oct)

The player, 20 Oct: "right now I can literally be at war with a civilization, send spaceships
into their system and they won't even attack. Let's make a combat system. For now it can be an
auto resolve one, tho let's do custom tactics choosable as modifiers that would modify the damage
inflicted and damage received."

## Why they do not attack now (the new version)

- A civilization we attack never builds a warship: it only arms for a war of its own
  (`planWarAI`, auto.ts). Attacked, it sends what it has to the one settlement of its own nearest
  our capital (the war's front) and digs in there; anywhere else, nothing comes.
- A settlement fires only with Defence Grids: without one it cannot touch a ship in its sky.
- Only warships take part in a battle (`battles`, conflict.ts): a probe or a settler at an
  enemy's star is never fired on.
- Losses never show until the end: a side's fire is spread over the other's hulls by what each
  has left, so every ship loses the same share each turn and all of them are lost in the same
  turn.
- A battle shows only as a log line; `state.battles` is written every turn, never shown, and
  never trimmed.

## The player's answers (20 Oct)

1. Tactics: **pick from a set**, per fleet, changeable any turn.
2. **The new version only** (at /next/, where the others have real fleets). Today's game stays as
   it is.
3. **Strike back**: in a war we begin, they build warships, hunt our ships in their space, and
   once strong enough besiege and storm our nearest settlement, as we can theirs; the same in the
   others' wars with each other.
4. **Every fight**: a fleet fights by its tactic in battles, sieges, assaults, raids and swarm
   fights.

## The rules

### Tactics (`src/game/data/tactics.ts`, `Fleet.tactic`, absent = Hold the line)

| Tactic | Dealt | Taken | Also |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hold the line | ×1 | ×1 | as every fleet fights now |
| Close in | ×1.5 | ×1.5 | |
| Stand off | ×0.5 | ×0.5 | holds no siege; cannot storm or raid; not dug in |
| Escort | ×0.75 | ×1 | its warships take the fire meant for its unarmed ships |

Grounded: close in, a laser's spot is small and a slug has no time to be dodged, both ways; at
the edge of range, the light-seconds between them let a jinking ship slip a beam or a slug, both
ways. Fortified (dug in) multiplies as before, but not while standing off.

Built, Stand off first took ×0.35 (as offered): it then won every fight, four Wardens standing
off beating five holding the line, so it takes ×0.5 like what it deals. Between two fleets Close
in and Stand off now set how fast a fight goes, not who wins it; what makes them matter is what
no battle silences (a settlement's guns), a swarm's odds, a siege, help on its way, and luck
(fewer turns, fewer draws).

### A battle, a round a turn

- Wherever two civilizations at war stand at one star (ships stopped there, or a settlement), and
  one of them can fire, they fight, once a turn, all at once, as now (`battlesTurn`): each side's
  fire from what stood at the start, on a draw (0.6 to 1.4), × FIRE (0.5).
- **Every ship is a target**, warships and unarmed alike (a Kinetic Lance in flight is not).
- **Every settlement fires** with what it has, its people's mining lasers and mass drivers turned
  on the ships in its sky: 0.5 + 0.075 a person (at most 6) without a single Defence Grid, and its
  Grids' 8 each on top, as now. Settlements are not hurt in a battle (sieges and assaults are how
  they fall).
- **Fire falls on ships one at a time**: the least hull left first, what destroys one going on to
  the next; an Escort's unarmed ships last. So ships are lost one by one, and a side's fire falls
  as it loses them.
- A ship's fire is its strength against ships, × dug in if fortified, × its fleet's tactic; the
  fire that reaches it × its own fleet's tactic.
- A round a turn whatever the pace, as before: a quicker pace gives more say in a battle.

### Sieges and storming, at any settlement, by either side

- A settlement is under siege while warships of a civilization at war with its owner hold its
  star (not standing off) and none of its owner's warships are there (`Colony.siege`, by whom and
  how many turns). Its owner's stores and will run down while it holds, and the besiegers carry
  off what the blockade drains, as now.
- Held three turns (and its walls broken by a Breacher), it can be stormed, as now: by either side
  of any war, at any settlement, not only by the side that began it at its front. The front stays
  as where the one who began it goes first.
- A save at war keeps its siege: format 5 moves a war's siege onto the settlement it was held at
  (`tools/saves/70c2d07-seed1000-turn43-siege-next.json.gz`, a war under way, made with the build
  before).

### The others at war (`planWarAI`), and our autoplayer

- **Arms** while at war, whoever began it: Wardens (an Aegis once it can build one), until its
  warships outweigh half again what threatens it; one that cannot build a warship learns Orbital
  Defense first.
- **Hunts** enemy ships at its own stars: its warships go for the strongest intrusion once they
  and the settlement there outweigh it half again; otherwise they gather dug in, at the threatened
  settlement if it is its capital, else at the capital, and it arms.
- **Picks a tactic** for each fleet where it fights: Escort with settlers aboard; Close in at
  half again the enemy's fire there or more; else Hold the line where it must hold a siege or is
  dug in at home, and Stand off anywhere else.
- **Withdraws** a fleet outmatched two to one, away from its own capital, to its capital (where
  its Shipyard mends it).
- **Strikes back**: with no intruders left and its warships half again what stands at our
  settlement nearest it, it goes there, holds the siege, and storms it when it can (razes, far
  down the Hunger's path).
- **Makes peace** in a war it began once it has lost a settlement in it, as well as after 30 turns
  or with its warships gone, as now.
- Our autoplayer (the harness) defends the same way, wherever enemy ships come.

### Every other fight

- A swarm's: our fleet's attack × dealt, the hurt to its ships × taken; Escort moves the unarmed's
  hurt onto its warships. The same draws in the same order, so Hold the line plays as before.
- A raid and an assault: force × dealt, losses × taken; Stand off cannot raid or storm.
- A siege of a neighbour without a civilization of its own (an old save's): their fire × taken;
  Stand off holds none.

### What the first forced wars showed (20 Oct)

A forced war at turn 40 (`--war=40`, warlike) first ended in four of twelve games with every
settlement of ours taken within twenty turns: early on all of them are at one star, so they were
besieged together, each drained 12 energy a turn, and all were stormed in one turn; the attacked
side's fleet flew home and back as its odds changed; and its takings counted as Hunger acts. So:
one blockade a star (12 energy, however many settlements are there), one storm a star a turn
(the longest held first), a counter-attack under way keeps on while it is at least half what
stands there, the side a war fell on takes with a cause of its own ("They began this war": no
Taint), our warlike autoplayer makes peace in a war it began once it is losing (as the others
do), and after a war each side's warships come home holding the line again.

## What the player sees

- **The fleet's panel**: a Tactic row (four buttons, each with what it does); with an enemy at
  its star, a Battle section: both sides (ships, hulls, tactics, settlements' fire), last turn's
  exchange, and what each tactic would deal and take this turn.
- **A settlement or a star under siege**: by whom, the turn of the siege, Storm and Raze where
  ours.
- **The war card** storms the settlement we besiege (the furthest siege), not only the front.
- **The log**: a line for every battle we are in, each turn, with what we dealt and took and what
  each side lost; `state.battles` kept for 20 turns, with both sides' losses.
- The fleet list shows a fleet's tactic. The galaxy map marks ✕ a star where we fought last turn
  or a siege of ours, or on ours, holds. The Manual's war and fleet paragraphs.
